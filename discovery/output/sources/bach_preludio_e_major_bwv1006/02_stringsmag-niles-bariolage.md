"Bariolage" is a French term for rapid string-crossing between open and stopped strings, used across musical genres from Bach to bluegrass. Two essential techniques for mastering this method:

**Large-muscle motion from the shoulder** involves changing arm angles to accommodate different strings. The G string requires a higher arm position, with progressive drops for D, A, and E strings. While shoulders can manage bariolage at moderate speeds, faster passages require different mechanics.

**Small-muscle wrist motion** becomes necessary for rapid two-string toggling. Initial practice: hold the wrist steady with the opposite hand while rocking up and down, then progress to the violin with simple back-and-forth motions between adjacent strings.

Three musical examples:
1. **"Devil's Dream"**: Uses wrist circles with separate bows between adjacent strings
2. **Bach Allegro** (Suzuki Book 8, measures 16-19): Transitions from wrist motion to arm motion when slurs are added
3. **Bach Preludio from Partita No. 3 in E major**: Introduces three-string crossing requiring alternating wrist and arm motions

The article emphasizes knowing when to apply each technique rather than relying on one approach for all bariolage passages.
