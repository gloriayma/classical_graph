**Allegro ma non troppo section:** The opening is "lonely with a 4-note timpani motive" that gradually expands orchestrally. The violin entrance is "newly self-conscious in its solitude." The opening violin passage features "rising octave leaps" where "each note in the scale must first descend an octave down," demonstrating vulnerability. The violin reaches high G multiple times but "loses courage on the third and cascades to the lower depths." The movement's substance is like "a Portobello mushroom trying to have the substance of meat" while maintaining "the fragility of feta." The development includes "a heart wrenching G minor section" with "a three note figure in the solo violin part which struggles by rising half-steps."

**Larghetto section:** Described as "the most sincere, earnest, and vulnerable" movement, it features "hollow whispered high notes of the beginning" that "descend into the throatier ranges." The writing maintains "hushed dynamics more like humming or murmuring than speaking aloud."

**Rondo section:** The finale is "entirely unconcerned with anything that isn't fun" and "robust," with constant sixteenth-note accompaniment figures and the return of earlier thematic material recontextualized as playful rather than insecure.
