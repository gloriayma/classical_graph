The piece features an exposed solo-violin arpeggio entrance after the orchestra's three-and-a-half-minute introduction.

**Finger and Hand Management:** "Keep your fingers light and watch the tension." Establishing the octave shape between first and fourth fingers (or first and third in higher registers) requires adjusting finger distance based on fingerboard position.

**Bow Distribution:** "Allow a little more bow-arm weight on the lower of the two strings involved." Lower pitches should serve as tuning references for upper notes. Vibrato originates from the first finger on the lower string.

**Practice Strategy:** Rather than repetitive interval drilling, work with specific études sharing similar bowing and arpeggiation patterns.

The traditional resources include Kreutzer's Études Nos. 24 and 25, which feature "slurred bowings and arpeggiations they share with the Beethoven." Walker also created "Your Grandad's Clothes," an étude-duet emphasizing varied intervals and intonation work with open string references.

The article emphasizes that octave mastery applies across the violin repertoire's double-stop techniques.
