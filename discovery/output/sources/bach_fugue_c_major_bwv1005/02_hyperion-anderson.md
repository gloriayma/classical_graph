The sonata opens with an elegiacally expressive Adagio whose writing embraces one-part to four-part texture. This leads to an extensive Fuga spanning 354 bars, derived from the Pentecostal antiphon 'Veni Sancte Spiritus'. The movement presents substantial contrapuntal challenges and intimidating technical requirements for performers.

The third movement, a Largo in F major, offers lyrical and serene melody providing contrast to the preceding Fuga's density. The work concludes with a spirited and brilliantly coloured Allegro assai where "Bach, once more, leads the performer and his instrument towards the limits of possibility."
