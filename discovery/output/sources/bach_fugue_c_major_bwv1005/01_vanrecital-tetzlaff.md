J.S. Bach's Sonatas and Partitas (completed before 1720) demonstrate ingenious exploitation of the multi-voice capabilities of violin. These works remained largely unknown until Joseph Joachim performed them in the nineteenth century.

The Adagio features pervasive dotted rhythm creating solemnity while highlighting how the amount of sound issuing from the instrument expands dramatically from single notes to quadruple-stop chordal texture.

The 354-measure fugue uses the chorale "Komm, heiliger Geist" as its subject with evenly descending chromatic half notes as countersubject. Midway through, Bach introduces an "al reverso" passage where subject and countersubject invert, with the countersubject now climbing chromatically.

The Largo provides lyric relief in two-voice texture with balanced phrasing, while the Allegro assai finale demonstrates implied part-writing spinning two- and three-voice textures at dazzling speed.
