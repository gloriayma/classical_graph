Pablo de Sarasate's Carmen Fantasy, Op. 25, is a fantasy on themes from Bizet's Carmen for violin and piano or orchestra, composed in 1881. The piece was dedicated to Joseph Hellmesberger Sr., with a piano version published in 1882.

The work incorporates the Aragonaise, Habanera, an interlude, Seguidilla, and the Gypsy Dance, with Sarasate selecting music specifically featuring Spanish character. A typical performance lasts approximately 12 minutes.

The Carmen Fantasy ranks among one of Sarasate's best-known works and frequently appears in violin competitions. Due to its delicate techniques and sanguineous passion inspired by the opera, it's considered one of the most challenging and technically demanding pieces for the violin.

Five-Movement Structure:

1. Allegro moderato: Features Aragonaise material with glissando, flageolet, and pizzicato techniques.
2. Moderato: Ornamented Habanera ("L'amour est un oiseau rebelle") from Act 1.
3. Lento assai: Carmen's mocking theme, ending in flageolets.
4. Allegro moderato: Seguidilla with pizzicato, trill, and glissando ornaments.
5. Moderato: Act 2 entertainment scene featuring rapid thirds, fast arpeggios, and virtuoso acceleration—the technically most demanding movement.
