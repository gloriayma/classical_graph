Henryk Wieniawski was a nineteenth-century violin virtuoso and composer. Although born into a musical family, he was the most celebrated member. His exceptional talent as a violinist was noticed early on by his first teacher, Jan Hornziel. In 1843, before he had reached 10 years of age, Wieniawski was admitted to the Paris Conservatoire. After winning awards and success in the Paris Conservatoire, Wieniawski went on to play several concerts in St Petersburg, Russia, where earned the praise of the court solo violinist, Vieuxtemps. After re-entering and completing his studies (this time including composition) at the Paris Conservatoire, Wieniawski embraced the career of a traveling virtuoso. He performed about 200 concerts in 3 years in collaboration with his brother, a pianist. Although they were generally received with enthusiasm, they definitely encountered criticism. As child prodigies, critics warned against excessive praise and claimed that they possessed nothing more than a gift of virtuosity. However, Wieniawski is notably unique as a violin virtuoso in that his compositional skills were comparable, if not exceeding of, his virtuosity.

As a violinist, he was often praised for his rich, warm tone and glowing temperament. Leopold Auer, who succeeded Wieniawski in his posts at St Petersburg, recalled that "He was entirely different from any other violinist of his day…in his manner of playing. Since his death no violinist has ever seemed able to recall him". His method of bowing was also something of high interest to violinists of the time; one of his most memorable and lasting impressions. His right elbow was always raised unusually high and he placed the most pressure on his index finger, creating quite a stiff bowhold. Albeit harsh criticism, this technique worked and allowed him to create a flawlessly effective "devil's staccato" (a virtuoso effect of playing many staccato notes in one bow on one string…I think…) when combined with a complete stiffening of the right arm.

As a composer, Wieniawski combined the technical advancements of Paganini with Romantic themes and Polish nationalism to produce his unique style. His major compositional contributions consist of his mazurkas, polonaises, and two concertos. The two concertos are indispensable works within violin repertoire, the first focusing (some say over-stressing) virtuosity and the second full of sweeping Romanticism, dedicated to violinist Sarasate. His collections of etudes are among the most musical and technically demanding studies for the violin.

Although Wieniawski produced large-scale works for the violin, his short pieces are none less intriguing. _Légende_ Op. 17 is a famous showpiece that Wieniawski wrote around 1860 for solo violin and orchestra (or piano reduction). This piece was an essential factor in securing his marriage with Isabella Hampton. Her parents initially disapproved of Wieniawski and completely forbid the engagement. However, after hearing his _Légende_ (which was dedicated to Isabella), they were so impressed and moved that they offered the couple their blessing. Naturally this piece emphasizes the Romantic theme of love. Characteristic of the Romantic movement, the love Wieniawski was experiencing was, at the time of composition, forbidden. In the first section of the piece, you can hear Wieniawski's longing in the phrasing, along with an emotional intensity in his use of extreme dynamics, slides, and octaves. His love is soon requited in the second section as the key changes to major and a seemingly endless (at least in my experience) sequence of double stops climaxes first in beautifully phrased open string chords, and then ultimately climaxes in tonic octaves and a fast-paced chromatic scale downward, resolving in the initial key area. The first section returns, this time muted and with different markings for color. This piece is an exemplary work of a nineteenth century virtuoso/composer that combines technical virtuosity with Romantic themes and emotional lyricism.
