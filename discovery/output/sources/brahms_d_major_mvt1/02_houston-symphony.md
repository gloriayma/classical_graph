title: "Style & Substance: Brahms' Violin Concerto in D major, Opus 77"
author: Houston Symphony program notes

Brahms composed this violin concerto during summer 1878 in collaboration with violinist Joseph Joachim, a lifelong friend and artistic partner. The work emerged from their shared belief that "music should have real meaning and substance rather than mere flashy virtuoso passages or stylistic novelties."

The concerto contains three movements instead of the four Brahms originally planned. The opening movement begins with "a simple, pentatonic phrase in the style of a horn call," establishing an idyllic pastoral atmosphere before introducing darker harmonic passages and a stern theme in D minor.

The second movement, described as "one of Brahms' loveliest creations," opens with a woodwind serenade in F major before the soloist enters with variations on this theme. A central section shifts to F-sharp minor, where "the soloist almost seems to speak through music."

The finale displays Hungarian influences—a nod to Joachim's heritage—featuring a "foot-stomping main theme" that alternates with contrasting episodes, including metrical shifts between duple and triple meters.

The concerto emphasizes substance over display. As one contemporary critic observed, it was "a concerto not for, but against the violin," though another countered that it was "for violin against orchestra—and the violin wins!" The solo part, though demanding, serves expression rather than mere virtuosity, with the orchestra playing an unusually prominent symphonic role.
