The concerto comprises three movements: Allegro non troppo, Adagio, and Allegro giocoso, ma non troppo.

Brahms' violin concerto showcases "double-stops, vigorous bowing, and the full range of violin pyrotechnics," distinguishing it from his more subtly virtuosic piano concertos. The composer drew inspiration from Magyar-influenced "Gypsy violin" music of his era, evident in the work's energetic phrasing and bowing style that "really digs into the strings."

The first movement follows traditional concerto structure, opening with formal introduction before the violin enters dramatically. The graceful second movement features an extended oboe introduction—notably, violinist Pablo de Sarasate famously refused to perform the work due to this waiting period. Once the solo violin enters, the movement gains considerable character. The finale, written in rondo form and returning to D major, represents "some of the most joyful music Brahms ever composed," offering "unalloyed exuberance."
