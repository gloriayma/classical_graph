"Brahms wrote the Violin Concerto in 1878 for his close friend, the great violinist Joseph Joachim, who advised him about the technical aspects of the solo part."

The opening presents "a D major theme that sounds like a distant horn call." Following this, "at a magical change to C major the oboe sings a lyrical theme over pulsating strings." The soloist enters with dramatic intensity, and "the second exposition, in contrast to the first, is a dialectic between peace and unrest." The development includes "a new tranquilo idea in the solo instrument" that "transforms into something ferocious."

This movement (the Adagio) "begins with a hymnlike theme, featuring a serene oboe melody that the violin in fact never gets to play." The violin enters "not with an echo of the oboe theme but a variation of it." The middle section features "elegant garlands of notes that continue to the end."

"All of Brahms's concertos end in his Hungarian/Gypsy mode, and this one is the most uproarious of the lot." Structured "more or less in sonata-rondo form, the rollicking leading theme returning regularly," the finale features "delight, all the way to a breathtaking coda."
