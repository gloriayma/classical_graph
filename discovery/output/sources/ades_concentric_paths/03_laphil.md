title: "Violin Concerto, 'Concentric Paths' by Thomas Adès"
author: LA Phil program note
---

Adès created this work in 2005 as a co-commission involving the Los Angeles Philharmonic Association, with support from Lenore and Bernard Greenberg, alongside the Berlin Festspiele. The piece runs approximately 20 minutes.

The concerto follows a three-movement design titled Rings, Paths, and Rounds. Rather than the traditional fast-slow-fast balance, Adès inverts expectations by extending the central slow movement to exceed the combined length of both outer movements, forming what he describes as "a triptych." The orchestration includes substantial woodwind and brass sections alongside strings and solo violin, with an array of percussion instruments.

The opening movement presents "sheets of unstable harmony in different orbits," featuring restless arpeggios that echo Berg and Ligeti's violin concertos. The expansive slow movement unfolds through overlapping cycles that "clash, sometimes violently," anchored by grave chaconne-like repetitions.

The work premiered September 4, 2005 in Berlin. The LA Phil presented its U.S. premiere on February 10, 2006, with violinist Anthony Marwood and Adès conducting.
