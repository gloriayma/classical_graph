Saint-Saëns composed this work in March 1880 for Pablo de Sarasate, with the premiere occurring January 2, 1881 in Paris. The instrumentation includes solo violin with pairs of woodwinds, brass, three trombones, timpani, and strings.

**First Movement:** The composer employs the standard fast-slow-fast structure. "The opening theme is almost obsessed with a turn figure made of half-steps," establishing a serious character. Like Mendelssohn's violin concerto, the soloist enters immediately without orchestral introduction. Saint-Saëns demonstrates orchestral sophistication through strategic instrument coloring—woodwinds, then horns, then trumpets punctuate the main theme with increasing brilliance. The movement follows straightforward sonata form with "a sweetly expressive second theme expressed in delicate triplets."

**Slow Movement:** The violinist performs "a gently rocking barcarolle, while woodwind solos surround it with zephyr breezes." The soloist and winds exchange roles throughout this intimate, song-like section that "never lifts its voice."

**Finale:** Following a slow introduction, a "bright staccato tune with hints of Spanish color" emerges—reflecting the composer's Spanish travels and the work's dedicatee. A more passionate secondary theme contrasts with the energetic Spanish-inflected material that ultimately dominates.
