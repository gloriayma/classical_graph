The liner notes describe how Sarasate commissioned this 1880 work, which followed Saint-Saëns' other concertos. The composer developed "the ability to write instantly memorable melodies" and employed "a colourful, romantic harmonic style, with modal inflections."

The first movement features a lyrical second subject in E major rather than the expected G major, creating a "magical world" contrasting with the opening's passion.

The second movement is a barcarolle in B-flat major, described as "extremely remote" with chamber-like intimacy between violin and woodwind voices. A notable passage uses harmonics accompanied by clarinet for "a strikingly original effect."

The finale begins with an abrupt shift to E minor via dramatic recitative-style dialogue. It possesses "a bright, march-like character" and is "the most extended, complex movement of the three," featuring multiple memorable themes and a chorale that returns triumphantly.

The notes emphasize that "the element of virtuosity" integrates throughout rather than appearing in discrete passages, allowing technical display to "add drama, excitement, or a decorative quality" as needed.
