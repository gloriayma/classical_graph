**Title:** Violin Concerto, op.47, D minor

**Composer:** Jean Sibelius (1865-1957)

**Instrumentation:** Solo violin, two flutes, two oboes, two clarinets, two bassoons, four horns, two trumpets, three trombones, timpani and strings.

## The Composer's Struggle

Sibelius faced significant personal challenges during the concerto's creation. A friend wrote to his wife Aino that "Jean can only be saved by the efforts of those closest to him" as he struggled with the temptations of Helsinki's social scene. Aino occasionally had to retrieve him from taverns so he could focus on completing the work.

## Revision and Relocation

Following the 1903 premiere, Sibelius grew dissatisfied with the original version. Aino encouraged him to relocate to a country villa in 1904, where his life stabilized. He successfully revised the concerto in 1906.

## Musical Structure

**First Movement:** Features a "high, lyrical first theme" contrasting with a "brooding yet forceful second" theme, both incorporated into a brilliant solo cadenza.

**Second Movement:** A romance movement where a melancholy opening contrasts with darker middle section material, known for its poignant emotional depth.

**Third Movement:** A rondo finale styled as a warm, Gypsy-influenced movement with "bright spirits and rich, shady orchestration."

Sir Donald Tovey ranked it among "one of the three most attractive concertos ever written."
