"The opening 'cadenza' for the unaccompanied violin sounds improvisatory and declamatory, beginning, in the instrument's sultry lowest range and progressing through slides, trills, octave passages, and harmonics, all the while calling for the kinds of changes and bending of tempo so characteristic of Gypsy music."

The accompaniment enters unexpectedly during the cadenza's conclusion. Subsequently, the violin and piano together present the dancelike main section, which develops earlier cadenza ideas and introduces two additional themes—"a sprightly patter first given to the piano and a swaggering theme marked 'grandiose.'"
