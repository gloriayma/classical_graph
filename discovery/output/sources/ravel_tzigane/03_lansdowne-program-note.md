Ravel is renowned for his elegance and orchestral craftsmanship, evident in works such as Mother Goose, Daphnis et Chloé and Boléro. He was also a master orchestrator, famously arranging works like Mussorgsky's Pictures at an Exhibition. Tzigane was inspired by Hungarian violinist Jelly d'Arányi. Drawing on Hungarian folk idioms and virtuosic traditions from Paganini and Liszt, the piece demands extraordinary technical skill. The work opens with an extended solo violin cadenza, initially confined to a single string, before expanding into a rhapsodic structure with dramatic orchestral interplay.

What is Ravel's Tzigane?

Tzigane is a virtuosic rhapsody for violin and orchestra composed by Maurice Ravel in 1924. It was written to showcase the fiery, improvisational style of Hungarian Romani folk music.

When was Tzigane composed and premiered?

Ravel composed Tzigane in 1924, and it was premiered in London on April 26, 1924 by violinist Jelly d'Arányi, to whom the work is dedicated.

How long is Tzigane?

A typical performance of Tzigane lasts approximately 10 to 12 minutes.

What should I listen for in Tzigane?

"The opening features the solo violin unaccompanied for nearly three minutes, showcasing extraordinary technical demands before the orchestra enters." Listen for the increasingly wild and energetic passages that build to a thrilling conclusion.

What is notable about Ravel in connection with this work?

Ravel was inspired to write Tzigane after hearing violinist Jelly d'Arányi play Hungarian folk tunes at a dinner party. Despite not being of Hungarian heritage, Ravel captured the spirit of Romani music with remarkable authenticity.
