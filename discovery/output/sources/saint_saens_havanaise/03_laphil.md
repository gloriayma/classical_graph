According to the LA Phil website, the piece was composed in 1887 for Cuban violinist Rafael Diaz Albertini. "Though it plays deftly with the habanera rhythm of Cuba, Saint-Saëns reportedly got the melodic idea for it staring into a fire on a cold night in Brest, listening to the crackling wood burn."

The composition is characterized as "a sunny, winsome piece" despite its later adaptation as the main theme for Roman Polanski's 1999 thriller The Ninth Gate, where it took on darker qualities.
