Heinrich Wilhelm Ernst was born in 1812 in Moravia and began studying at the Vienna Conservatory at thirteen.

Even though we don't hear much of his music today, he was one of the great virtuoso players of the nineteenth century, mentioned in the same breath as Paganini (who became both his idol – and rival).

This is the last work from the last set of studies he published, the 6 Polyphonic Études. In it, he uses the melody of the Irish folksong "The Last Rose of Summer" as the basis for some jaw-dropping virtuosity, including a filigreed version of the melody accompanied by simultaneous left-handed pizzicato.

These studies are "polyphonic" – meaning "multiple voices" – so listen to hear how Ernst makes the violin sound as if it is playing a duet – and sometimes a quartet! – with itself.
