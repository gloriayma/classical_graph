Marie Cornaz explains that Vieuxtemps "finished composing the 5th Violin Concerto in the spring of 1861" as "an exam piece for the students in the advanced class of the Belgian violinist Hubert Léonard at the Brussels Conservatory."

Ray Iwazumi notes that having "a choice of two cadenzas" may have been "intended as a feature of its purpose as a competition piece." He characterizes them differently: Cadenza No. 1 functions as "a fantasy of the solo violin exposition," while Cadenza No. 2 comes "across as an elaborate variation of the opening tutti exposition."

The work "was at once successful," Cornaz states. Even "Hector Berlioz, often very critical, immediately praised the 5th Violin Concerto."

Iwazumi argues that "the violin technique called upon to play Vieuxtemps's Concerto in a minor has probably not changed that drastically since it was written."

A "complete working manuscript" of Eugène Ysaÿe's arrangement of Cadenza No. 1 was discovered and will appear as a first edition in the new Henle edition's appendix.
