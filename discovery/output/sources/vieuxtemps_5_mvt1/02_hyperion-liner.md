The work was "written in 1858–9 at the request of his friend Hubert Léonard, as the test-piece for a competition at the Brussels Conservatory."

Originally structured differently, "there was no slow movement, only the first movement and finale linked by a cadenza," though Vieuxtemps later added the Adagio for structural balance. The piece is typically performed continuously without breaks.

The first movement features an orchestral introduction establishing three themes in A minor, followed by the violin entering with "reflective rising arpeggios" before developing virtuosic passages. A cadenza follows, described as "a magnificent polyphonic meditation" rather than merely a display vehicle.

The Adagio contains the titular 'Grétry' reference—a melody adapted from André Grétry's opera Lucille. The violin performs "an almost operatic solo against a hushed pizzicato accompaniment," eventually presenting the borrowed melody "in C major" with "touching pathos."

The finale functions as "more of a bravura coda than a movement in its own right," alluding to earlier themes while driving toward closure.
