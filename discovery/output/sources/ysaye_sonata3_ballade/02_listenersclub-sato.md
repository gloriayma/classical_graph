At the turn of the twentieth century, the Belgian violinist Eugène Ysaÿe (1858-1931) earned recognition as "The King of the Violin." Sir Henry Wood praised his tone quality and noted that "He seemed to get more color out of a violin than any of his contemporaries." Wood also observed Ysaÿe's masterful approach to rubato, saying "Whenever he stole time from one note, he faithfully paid it back within four bars."

Ysaÿe composed Six Sonatas for solo violin in July 1923, following J.S. Bach's model. These works showcase demanding contrapuntal passages that expand the instrument's technical possibilities. Each sonata was dedicated to a contemporary violinist. Sonata No. 3 in D Minor, subtitled "Ballade," was dedicated to Romanian violinist George Enescu.

This single-movement work divides into two sections. The introduction (Lento molto sostenuto) functions as an impassioned recitative containing "falling half steps which suggest lamenting sighs" and whole-tone scale implications. The subsequent Allegro in 3/8 time features "spirited dotted rhythms, double and triple stops, and nervous leaps in register," building tension toward a virtuosic coda.

Japanese violinist Shunsuke Sato performs this excerpt from his 2004 recording of the complete Ysaÿe Sonatas.
