"The idea of writing this set of sonatas came after Ysaÿe had heard Joseph Szigeti give a stunning performance of Bach's G minor solo sonata (BWV1001)."

The composition was inspired by Bach's work, though Ysaÿe recognized modern developments in both musical language and technique. He sketched all six sonatas within a single day in July 1923, with publication following in 1924.

Joseph Szigeti (1892–1973), born in Budapest with his professional debut in Berlin in 1905, was influenced by Busoni. He premiered Prokofiev's first Violin Concerto in 1924 and maintained traditional bowing technique while demonstrating exceptional technical mastery and musical sensitivity.

The opening Grave establishes the collection's characteristics: "much double-, treble- and quadruple-stopping, often with one of the constituent notes to be accented" along with chromatic passages and tremolo effects.

The Fugato features "an innocent, Bachian subject" hidden within rhythmic structures and proclaimed in sextuple-stopping. The third movement in B-flat major is marked "amabile and scherzoso," employing Baroque structural conventions. The Finale functions as "a weighty gigue" with contrasting textures and thematic returns.
