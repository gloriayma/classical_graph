## Paul Griffiths (ECM Zehetmair recording liner notes, 2004)

Bach's sonatas and partitas and Paganini's caprices are the only masterpieces for unaccompanied violin before Ysaÿe's six sonatas. Ysaÿe composed these works in 1923/24 at his Belgian coastal home, carrying Bach and Paganini's influence within his creative mind.

**Sonata No. 1 (G minor):** a shadow sonata after Bach's first, following a similar slow-fast-slow-fast structure with a fugato in second position. Ysaÿe reaches Bach not through historical reconstruction but through presence and continuity. The work features four-note chords and motivic insistence on rising minor seconds. A passage played "sul ponticello, near the bridge" creates shadowy effects. The movement concludes with "a chime of distant light; a wild high minor tenth, like a scream; the same interval softened and put into the major; the inevitable and decisive fifth."

## Robin Stowell (Cambridge Companion to the Violin, 1992)

Ysaÿe's Six Sonatas Op. 27 (1924) represent a further peak in the development of the genre. Each of the six sonatas is dedicated to and written stylistically appropriate for a celebrated contemporary violinist: Szigeti, Thibaud, Enescu, Kreisler, Crickboom, and Quiroga.

Szigeti's Bach performances inspired Ysaÿe to initiate this project. The sonatas betray Bach's influence, not least in the complex polyphony and the tonality (G minor, the key of Bach's first unaccompanied sonata) of No. 1 and in the opening movement ("Obsession") of No. 2 in A minor, clearly inspired by the opening Preludio of Bach's E major partita.

**No. 1:** Features "ghostly tremolo sul ponticello coda of the Grave"
