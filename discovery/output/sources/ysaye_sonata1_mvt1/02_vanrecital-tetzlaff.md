Ysaÿe bridged the late Romantic era of virtuoso violinists and twentieth-century composers. He wrote his Six Sonatas after hearing violinist Josef Szigeti in 1923, with each dedicated to contemporary players. The first sonata honors Szigeti's scholarly style.

The opening movement employs triple- and quadruple-stop chordal texture reminiscent of Bach's slow movements, alternating with rhapsodic technical passages ending in tremolo double stops sul ponticello.

The Fugato second movement features two-voice fugal-type entries with contrasting episodes, showcasing six-note chords at its conclusion—an extreme violin technique demand.

The third movement, marked amabile, presents a coy triplet figure imitated between the top and bottom layers. One contrasting section uses parallel fourths and fifths reminiscent of Debussy.

The final Allegro fermo movement draws from Bach's Gavotte rhythmically, employing hemiola patterns of Baroque rhythm while serving modern virtuosic demands.
