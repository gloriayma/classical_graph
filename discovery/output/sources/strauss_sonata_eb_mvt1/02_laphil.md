Strauss composed this Violin Sonata in 1887 when he was only 23 years old, premiering it the following year. By that time, he had already created "a substantial body of abstract instrumental music, including two symphonies, two concertos, two piano trios, a piano quartet, a string quartet, and a cello sonata, as well as dozens of songs."

The work displays "idiomatic virtuosity" characteristic of Strauss's understanding of both piano and violin, instruments he played himself. The outer movements convey noble aspiration in E-flat major—the same key Strauss would later use for *Ein Heldenleben* and which Beethoven had chosen for his "Eroica" Symphony.

The opening movement uses flexible meter changes for different thematic material, even featuring "the two instruments playing in different meters at one point." The Finale begins with a hushed piano prelude before introducing an energetic theme closely tied to the first movement's opening.

The middle movement, marked Andante cantabile, proved especially popular. Described as "rapt, long-breathed," this Improvisation section achieved such acclaim that "Strauss allowed it to be published separately." Romantic ardor permeates the "lush lyricism" throughout, reflecting the year Strauss first met soprano Pauline de Ahna, whom he would marry.
