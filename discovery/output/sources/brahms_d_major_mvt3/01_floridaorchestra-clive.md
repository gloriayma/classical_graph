The concerto features "double-stops, vigorous bowing, and the full range of violin pyrotechnics," distinguishing it from Brahms' more subtle piano concertos. The writing reflects "Magyar-inspired music known in his day as 'Gypsy violin,'" evident in "the energy and the style of phrasing, and in bowing that really digs into the strings." The finale is "unalloyed exuberance— some of the most joyful music Brahms ever composed." It's written in rondo form and "returns the concerto to its original D major key." Passages "seem to invite the tossing of hair and stamping of feet."
