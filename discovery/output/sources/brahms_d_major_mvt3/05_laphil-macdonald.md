Brahms made his choice of a violin concerto "doubly plain by choosing the same key as Beethoven, D major" and followed classical sonata form conventions. Brahms worked closely with dedicatee Joseph Joachim to create "a test of the player's technique and musicianship" while avoiding "unmotivated display."

Regarding the finale's character: "boisterous lilt is a tribute to Joachim's Hungarian birth," though other non-Hungarian themes appear throughout. The movement features "a dynamic rising scale in octaves and a beautifully lyrical episode" with metric shifts from 2/4 to 3/4, concluding with a distinctive "6/8 pulse with heavy offbeats."
