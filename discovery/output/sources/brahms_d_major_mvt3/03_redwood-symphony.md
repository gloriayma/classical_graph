The finale, **Allegro giocoso**, features a sprightly double-stopped melody with a gypsy-based rhythm.

The first movement "allows the soloist to display beautifully florid, expressive lines, requiring consummate skill"; the work includes "a cadenza near the end" of the first movement. The second movement features the soloist playing "variations upon this line or reacts to it." The finale presents "double-stopped melody" with "a long, dramatic coda shared between the orchestra and soloist."

Conductor Hans von Bülow called it "a concerto against the violin" and violinist Bronislaw Huberman described it as "a concerto for violin against orchestra–and the violin wins!"
