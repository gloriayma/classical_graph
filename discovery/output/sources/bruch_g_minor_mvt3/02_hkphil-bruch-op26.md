Composer: Max Bruch (1838-1920)

Work: Violin Concerto no. 1 in G minor, op. 26
- Vorspiel: Allegro moderato
- Adagio
- Finale: Allegro energico

Artists: Wilson Ng (Conductor), Zia Shin (Violin)

The concerto was completed in 1864 but premiered two years later on April 24, 1866, in Koblenz, Germany. After revisions suggested by Hungarian violinist Joseph Joachim, the work achieved significant success following Joachim's 1868 premiere and became Bruch's most enduring legacy, despite his frustration with its overshadowing of his other compositions.

The three movements flow continuously. The opening features "the violin emerging from the briefest of hymn-like introductions," establishing the soloist's dominance. The second movement showcases the violin's "rich middle register in a melody of unusual beauty." The finale presents a "virile, angular Gypsy-style theme," reflecting Joachim's Hungarian heritage.
