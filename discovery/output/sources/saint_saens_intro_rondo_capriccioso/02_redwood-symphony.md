Camille Saint-Saëns began composing at age three and debuted as a concert pianist at 11. He became known for elegant, perfectly-proportioned works, eventually composing nearly 300 pieces over 86 years. Though initially considered progressive, he later became reactionary, opposing works by Debussy and Stravinsky.

In the 1850s, Saint-Saëns met Spanish violinist Pablo Sarasate, a child prodigy who debuted at eight. Saint-Saëns composed his Violin Concerto in A major (1859) for the young Sarasate at age 15. When Sarasate turned 19, Saint-Saëns wrote the Introduction and Rondo Capriccioso in 1863, though it remained unpublished until 1870. Sarasate popularized the piece throughout Europe and America, leading Bizet and Debussy to create their own arrangements.

The work features a slow introduction marked "Andante malinconico" ("melancholy") that builds gradually toward a mini-cadenza. The rondo theme, marked "Allegro ma non troppo," displays Spanish character with "huge leaps and brilliant arpeggios." A contrasting lyrical section appears "con morbidezza" ("with tenderness"), where the soloist plays in 2/4 time against the orchestra's 6/8. The piece concludes with an oboe statement, triple-stopping passages, and "a brilliant coda, undoubtedly written as a showcase for Sarasate's virtuosity."
